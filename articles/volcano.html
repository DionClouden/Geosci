01 / 07 · Atmosphere

Atmosphere

A big eruption throws a column of ash and gas upward. How high it gets decides whether it's a local problem or a global one.

02 / 07 · Ash and gases

Ash cloud and volcanic gases

The plume is a mix of rock fragments and gas. Pick an ingredient to see what it does.

03 / 07 · Pyroclastic flows

Pyroclastic flows

Sometimes the column collapses. An avalanche of hot gas and rock hugs the ground and races downhill, faster than you can drive.

Up to about 700 km/h and 1,000 °C. Nothing in its path survives.

04 / 07 · Magma chamber

Magma chamber

Under the volcano, molten rock collects in a pocket. Dissolved gas forms bubbles as it sits, like a shaken fizzy drink. Turn up the pressure.

05 / 07 · Conduit

Conduit

A conduit is the pipe magma climbs on its way to the surface, from the mantle, past the chamber, up to the crater. What happens on the way depends on how sticky the magma is.

06 / 07 · Mantle melting

Mantle melting

The mantle is solid rock, so where does magma come from? Rock doesn't need to get hotter to melt. It just needs less pressure. Lift this block of mantle and watch.

07 / 07 · Darkness

Darkness

Below about 100 km there is no light, no air and no sound. This is where every eruption starts, slowly, over thousands of years. Now put it all together.
