GEOSCI / ROCK CYCLE

Life of a grain of sand

Follow one grain of quartz from a beach to the seabed, deep underground, into solid rock, up into a mountain, down a river and back to the beach. The grain barely changes. Everything around it does. It stays in the same place on your screen while the world moves around it.

Scroll down ↓

01 / 08 · The beach

Where the sand is

Beaches hold more loose sand than almost anywhere, but every grain there is a survivor. Soft minerals get ground to clay or dissolve. Quartz is hard and chemically stable, so it lasts. Drag the slider to see who is left.

02 / 08 · The seabed

Out to sea

Waves and currents carry the grain offshore. Big, heavy grains drop out first while mud drifts furthest, so the seabed sorts itself. Change the energy and watch it happen.

03 / 08 · Buried

Buried

Sediment keeps raining down. Each new layer buries the grain deeper, where it gets hotter and is squeezed harder. Drag the depth to see what the grain feels.

04 / 08 · Sandstone

Turning to stone

Squeezing and mineral-rich water turn loose sand into rock, in steps called compaction and cementation. The grain does not change. The gaps around it do. Zoom in and run the clock.

05 / 08 · Uplift

Pushed up

Colliding tectonic plates fold and lift whole layers of rock into mountains. The grain rises with its layer, from kilometres underground to high above the sea.

06 / 08 · The mountain

Coming apart

Now exposed, the rock is attacked by ice, water and air. Water seeps into cracks, freezes, expands and pries the rock apart. Freeze and thaw it yourself.

07 / 08 · The river

Down the river

Freed grains wash into streams. How a particle travels depends on its size and how fast the water flows. Speed the river up and see what lifts.

08 / 08 · The rock cycle

Back to the beach

The river delivers the grain to the coast, and waves spread it back onto a beach. That loop is one path through the rock cycle. Follow the other paths it could have taken.
